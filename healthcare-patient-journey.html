01 / CONTEXT

Project Introduction

The business challenge

Hospital visits generate data across consultations, diagnostics, laboratory tests, treatment, and follow-up care. When these records are scattered across clinical systems, leaders cannot easily evaluate the complete patient journey or connect resource use with service quality.

This project examines a flat-file healthcare dataset to provide a unified analytical view for executives, department heads, and financial managers.

Objectives & scope

  • Track patient volume, repeat utilization, and departmental workload
  • Explore laboratory patterns and treatment outcomes
  • Compare treatment costs across departments and care types
  • Translate findings into questions and recommendations for decision-makers

Dataset: 20,000 visit records, 26 columns; January 1, 2025–June 25, 2026. Each row represents one recorded visit.

Actions & Analytical Workflow

01

Define business questions

Identified executive, clinical, and financial stakeholders and the decisions each dashboard should support.

Created Executive Overview, Clinical Performance & Outcome Quality, and Financial Distribution dashboards.

Compared matched reporting periods, departmental outcomes, treatment types, and costs rather than relying solely on headline totals.

Documented follow-up analyses of falling visit volume, abnormal results, readmissions, and treatment cost drivers.

05 / ANALYTICAL RESULTS

Key Findings

Visit demand

The documented first-half comparison shows 6,542 visits in 2025 versus 6,422 in 2026. The analysis flags this decline for further investigation, especially the February 2026 dip.

Returning patients

66.81% of the patients were categorized as returning. That highlights repeat service utilization, but does not on its own prove patient loyalty or continuity of care.

Clinical outcomes

71.26% of lab statuses were classified as abnormal (Low, High, or Critical). Readmitted was the largest single outcome category at 20.5%; deeper case-mix analysis is needed.

Treatment efficiency

Reported average treatment costs differed more by treatment type than by department. Length of stay did not show a simple proportional relationship with average cost.

06 / TAKEAWAYS

Final Results & Recommendations

Delivered

  • Documented dataset structure, business questions, and a dimensional modeling approach
  • Built three dashboard views for operational, clinical and financial analysis
  • Calculated and explained core visit, outcome and cost metrics
  • Prepared stakeholder-specific observations and follow-up recommendations

What leadership should investigate next

  • Validate the recent visit-volume decline with like-for-like time periods
  • Segment abnormal results and readmissions by diagnosis and department
  • Account for patient complexity before ranking clinical treatment effectiveness
  • Assess cost drivers by treatment type, rather than using length of stay alone
